# Contributors

This vocabulary is built from practitioner input. The people below shaped it — several of them
changed the framework itself, not just its contents.

## Framework changes

**Amanda Kollmorgan**
Identified that the intent axis assumed an actor, and that some of the most persistent AI risk has
none — "when it finally crashes and burns, there's no one to pin on the intent axis at all." This
added the **null / unattributable** intent value. Also contributed the **Ownership Drift** condition
and the distinction that decides its remediation: *offboarding is a process that failed; a lateral
move never had a process to begin with.* Her input merged two candidate entries into one rather than
adding a new persona.

**Purva Dublay** — *Application Security SME, AI Security Strategy*
Contributed two conditions beyond the OWASP Top 10: **Premature Reviewer Cuts / Role Displacement**
(cutting reviewers and control steps too early because the AI "usually works," eroding operational
resilience and institutional knowledge) and **executive governance failure**, now recorded as
**The Orphaned Model** — AI deployed without inventory, ownership, risk acceptance, auditability, or
incident response.

**Johnny Xmas** — *adversarial researcher*
Raised the planted-employee case from direct professional experience, contributing **The Plant** and
sharpening the project's insider-threat framing: a paycheck is no longer a guarantee of loyalty, and
frameworks under-treat the possibility that an employee was never loyal to begin with.

**A principal engineer** *(credit pending permission)*
Named the self-inflicted cost cluster: the **Needless Complexity** condition (elaborate agent setups
that burn many times the tokens for the same result), **Model Overkill** (using a far more powerful
and expensive model than the task requires), and the persona that follows from them,
**The Overengineer**.

## Also contributed

**Kate Turchin** — *Cybersecurity Marketing & GTM Leader*
Confirmed the thesis travels beyond hands-on security audiences, and identified the personas as a
natural basis for employee security-awareness training.

**Sharanya Mishra**
On the framing: looking at the human side of AI risks makes these conversations more practical.

**Anonymous** — a senior technologist whose account of running unattended agent testing in
production, fully instrumented for recovery, produced the **Preparedness** axis and the rule that
preparedness must be judged per impact class rather than as a blanket "we're covered."
